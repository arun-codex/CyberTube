# Cybersecurity: Zero to Advanced — Video-First Complete Learning Path

**Curriculum release:** 2026-11-20  
**Language:** English  
**Audience:** Absolute beginners, aspiring SOC analysts, defensive security engineers and authorized ethical-hacking learners.  
**Estimated workload:** 451 hours (video + self-study + labs; planning estimates, not video runtimes)  
**Modules:** 30  
**Cost:** Designed around free-to-watch YouTube videos and openly accessible references; some recommended lab platforms offer optional paid features, and some require registration.  
**Outcome:** Demonstrable foundational-to-advanced practical cybersecurity competency, not a promise of professional certification or employment.

## How to use this pack
1. Start with [Video and Free Resources](VIDEO_AND_FREE_RESOURCES.md), then Module 01.
2. Each module includes **five detailed lessons**, a direct YouTube resource, topic-specific YouTube discovery link, free references, a guided lab, assessment and instructor instructions.
3. Study 8–12 hours/week over approximately 45–56 weeks; accelerate or extend based on readiness.
4. Use no more than one primary video series at a time; seek targeted videos for topics it doesn't cover.
5. Complete a module's lab and write-up before progressing; rewatch and correct gaps when scoring below 80/100.
6. Preserve evidence in a private portfolio: redacted screenshots, timestamps, sources, observations, uncertainty and recommendations.
7. Choose emphasis: SOC/blue-team graduates prioritize Modules 08–19 and 24–30; ethical-hacking graduates add Modules 20–22 within authorized training labs.
8. Finish the capstone and job-readiness module. See [Capstone Brief](CAPSTONE_AND_ASSESSMENT.md) and [Weekly Plan](WEEKLY_STUDY_PLAN.md).

## Safety / permission rules
- Practice only on your own devices, authorized environments, or purpose-built training targets.
- Never use unknown live malware, attempt credential attacks on external users, target production systems, or upload confidential data to public services.
- Use safe sample files, synthetic logs, snapshots, simulated phishing, and tabletop ransomware exercises.
- For offensive concepts, focus on understanding, bounded lab validation, reporting and remediation.

# Capstone, Assessment Rubric and Portfolio Instructions

## Capstone: suspected phishing leading to suspicious authentication
A fictitious employee reports a phishing-style email. Synthetic authentication logs show unusual logins, and a sample endpoint event timeline indicates a suspicious child process. Learners must not send or execute live malicious payloads.

### Inputs to prepare
- One entirely fictitious phishing email, with example.com links only.
- 50–100 synthetic authentication events in CSV, including normal activity and suspicious patterns.
- 10–20 synthetic endpoint process events with timestamps and parent IDs.
- Optional safe PCAP generated by the learner in a private lab.

### Learner workflow
1. Validate scope and inventory artifacts; record all time zones.
2. Analyze email sender/display name, headers and URLs **as text only**.
3. Aggregate login results by account, timestamp and source; rule out common benign causes.
4. Correlate endpoint and identity signals into a single timestamped timeline.
5. Map observations to MITRE ATT&CK only when supported by evidence.
6. Assign severity and confidence, describe unanswered questions.
7. Recommend reversible containment, credential recovery, evidence preservation and stakeholder notification.
8. Create a 2-page technical incident report and 1-page executive summary.

### Grading (100 points)
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Scope, ethics, evidence handling | 15 |
| Timeline and correlation accuracy | 25 |
| Alternative explanations and confidence | 15 |
| Detection and response recommendations | 20 |
| Report structure, reproduction and sources | 15 |
| Five-minute spoken explanation | 10 |

**Pass:** At least 80 points with no scope/safety violations. Externally provided credentials or personal data must never appear in evidence artifacts.

## SOC portfolio minimum
- Windows login anomaly investigation.
- Phishing triage report.
- Network packet investigation.
- Malware or ransomware tabletop analysis.
- End-to-end SOC capstone case.

For every case, include scope, environment, sanitized source data, procedure, evidence, uncertainty, findings and recommended next steps. Do not publish raw sensitive logs.

## Guided hands-on lab (authorized environments only)

**Scenario:** Create a personal lab policy, threat model a fictitious company, and classify ten security events.

**Preparation:** Use a disposable virtual machine, synthetic logs, or the provider's sanctioned training environment. Take a VM snapshot first. Do not scan public systems, test accounts you do not own, deploy live malware, or use real credentials.

**Workflow:**
1. Write the objective and a brief scope statement, including owned hosts and permitted data.
2. Capture a clean baseline or load safe sample data; record time zone and source.
3. Complete the scenario using the relevant documented tools, recording each observation.
4. Separate facts from assumptions, benign explanations from malicious indicators, and confirmed from unconfirmed findings.
5. Recommend detection, mitigation, containment or escalation as appropriate; do not take destructive action.
6. Produce the required evidence artifact: **Threat model and authorization policy**. Remove tokens, personal identifiers and secrets before sharing.

## Practice exercises
1. **Recall:** Write plain-English definitions for all five learning topics.
2. **Analyze:** From a sanitized event set, identify three likely indicators, two benign alternatives and the missing evidence.
3. **Explain:** Draft a 150-word SOC analyst note containing observations, confidence, impact and recommended next step.
4. **Extend:** Design one safe improvement to logging, detection, or a prevention control.

## Guided hands-on lab (authorized environments only)

**Scenario:** Publish a portfolio of 5 sanitized lab reports and rehearse explaining a case in 5 minutes.

**Preparation:** Use a disposable virtual machine, synthetic logs, or the provider's sanctioned training environment. Take a VM snapshot first. Do not scan public systems, test accounts you do not own, deploy live malware, or use real credentials.

**Workflow:**
1. Write the objective and a brief scope statement, including owned hosts and permitted data.
2. Capture a clean baseline or load safe sample data; record time zone and source.
3. Complete the scenario using the relevant documented tools, recording each observation.
4. Separate facts from assumptions, benign explanations from malicious indicators, and confirmed from unconfirmed findings.
5. Recommend detection, mitigation, containment or escalation as appropriate; do not take destructive action.
6. Produce the required evidence artifact: **Job-ready portfolio and interview checklist**. Remove tokens, personal identifiers and secrets before sharing.
