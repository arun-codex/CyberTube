# Original Phrasebook — English for SOC and Ethical Hackers

> 88 original professional phrases. Adapt to your organisation’s approved policy; do not use examples as evidence of an actual incident.

## Introductions and confidence

- Hello, I am part of the security operations team.
- My role is to review security alerts.
- I am new to this project, so I may ask clarifying questions.
- Thank you for taking the time to speak with me.
- I will start with a short summary.
- Please let me know if I am speaking too quickly.
- Could you introduce the person responsible for this system?
- I am still learning the process, and I will confirm before acting.

## Clarity, pronunciation and readback

- Could you repeat that more slowly?
- Please spell the hostname letter by letter.
- Did you say zero or the letter O?
- Let me read the case ID back to you.
- The test IP is one nine two dot zero dot two dot ten.
- To confirm, the event occurred at fourteen oh five UTC.
- I heard the device name, but not the account name.
- I will pause between technical identifiers.

## Listening and clarification

- I want to make sure I understood correctly.
- Could you explain what you mean by unusual?
- Which system was affected?
- When did you first notice this?
- Was this action expected or unexpected?
- Do you know whether the user opened the attachment?
- What evidence supports that conclusion?
- Do you want a written update or a phone call?

## Alert triage

- We observed repeated failed authentication attempts.
- The source of those attempts is not yet confirmed.
- The current alert status is under investigation.
- The detection rule matched the sample event.
- We have not verified the user’s identity.
- The available logs do not establish a successful compromise.
- Please review the related endpoint events.
- I will add these findings to the ticket.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- According to the reviewed logs, the event occurred at 09:10 UTC.
- The information available so far is incomplete.
- The current hypothesis is unconfirmed.
- We have not found evidence of that action in the data reviewed.
- An absence of evidence is not proof the event did not happen.
- This observation may indicate suspicious activity.
- We should not attribute the activity without more evidence.
- I will separate observations from assumptions.

## Escalation and incident updates

- I am escalating this ticket for additional review.
- The reason for escalation is the repeated alert pattern.
- Please confirm who will take ownership.
- The next requested action is to verify the asset owner.
- This update is preliminary and may change with new evidence.
- We will report any confirmed findings through the approved channel.
- Could you acknowledge receipt of this escalation?
- Please attach the relevant test evidence to the case.

## Email and chat etiquette

- Subject: Verification requested for unusual test sign-in
- I am writing to request confirmation about a simulated alert.
- For context, the system generated three notifications.
- The attached summary contains the relevant timestamps.
- Please respond with the expected activity status.
- Thank you for reviewing this request.
- I have added a brief summary to the incident thread.
- I will avoid posting sensitive data in the channel.

## Phone calls and handover

- Hello, this is the security operations team.
- Is now a suitable time for a short verification call?
- I do not need your password or one-time code.
- I will summarise our understanding before ending the call.
- The next shift will continue the investigation.
- The open questions are listed in the handover note.
- The assigned owner is awaiting confirmation.
- Please repeat the next action so we can verify the handoff.

## Meetings and disagreements

- The purpose of this meeting is to agree on the next steps.
- Let us review the evidence before drawing a conclusion.
- I see your point, but that finding is not yet verified.
- Could we use the term suspicious instead of malicious?
- Would anyone like to add information?
- Let us return to the agreed agenda.
- The action item is to verify the account owner.
- Who will take responsibility for this follow-up?

## Presentations and executive summaries

- First, I will explain what we observed.
- Next, I will discuss the possible impact.
- This diagram shows the reviewed event sequence.
- The most important point is the current uncertainty.
- In business terms, one test device requires further investigation.
- We have not confirmed data loss in the information reviewed.
- Our recommendation is to validate the affected account.
- I will finish with the decision that needs to be made.

## Job interviews and responsible disclosure

- In a training lab, I investigated a simulated security alert.
- My contribution was to organise the evidence and report it clearly.
- I used a structured method to explain the findings.
- I can show my original practice report and reflection.
- I have not worked on a production incident yet.
- I reported the finding through an authorised channel.
- I would not share confidential evidence without permission.
- The main lesson was to communicate uncertainty honestly.

## Daily phrasebook practice

1. Choose five phrases from one category.
2. Explain each phrase in your own easy English.
3. Say each phrase aloud slowly, then at normal pace.
4. Write two cybersecurity-specific dialogues using a synthetic case.
5. Rewrite one phrase for a manager and one for an analyst.
6. Verify that your claims are accurate and appropriately qualified.
