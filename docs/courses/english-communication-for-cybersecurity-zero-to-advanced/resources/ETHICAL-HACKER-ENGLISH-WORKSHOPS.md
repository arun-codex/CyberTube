# Ethical Hacker / Penetration Tester — English Communication Specialisation

**Placement:** Optional application workshop after Modules 15–30. This is about communication, permission, and reporting — not instructions for exploitation. No additional formal core module count is claimed.

## Workshop 1 — Explain scope and authorisation (45 minutes)
**Language:** permission, in scope, out of scope, approval, contact, deadline.
**Simulation:** A fictitious client wants a web assessment. Ask politely which system is in scope and where written permission is recorded. Do not perform any testing.
**Practice script:** “Could you confirm the approved scope and point of contact in writing? We will not access any unapproved assets.”
**Artifact:** 130-word scope-confirmation email.

## Workshop 2 — Explain a finding without overstating impact (45 minutes)
**Language:** observed, reproduced, possible impact, affected component, recommendation.
**Simulation:** You are given a fictional benign misconfiguration and only simulated screenshots.
**Practice script:** “We observed the following configuration in the authorised test environment. The potential impact requires validation.”
**Artifact:** 170-word factual finding description; label observed evidence versus possible risk.

## Workshop 3 — Responsible vulnerability disclosure (45 minutes)
**Language:** confidential, evidence, authorised contact, acknowledgement, mitigation.
**Simulation:** Write a respectful message to an imaginary security response team about a non-exploitative mock issue. Do not share real discoveries or vulnerability details outside authorised channels.
**Practice script:** “We would like to report a potential issue through your approved disclosure process. Please confirm a secure contact channel.”
**Artifact:** 150-word responsible-disclosure email.

## Workshop 4 — Client debrief and risk prioritisation (45 minutes)
**Language:** finding, severity, confidence, prioritise, remediation owner, follow-up.
**Simulation:** A nontechnical client asks which of three fictitious findings should be reviewed first.
**Practice script:** “Our recommendation is to prioritise this item for validation because it affects a critical business process.”
**Artifact:** Two-minute business-oriented voice briefing and six-bullet decision record.

## Workshop 5 — Write a concise report for a varied audience (45 minutes)
**Language:** executive summary, methodology, limitation, evidence, recommendation.
**Simulation:** Present a mock assessment that collected observations but did not perform intrusive tests.
**Artifact:** 1-page report with short summary, approved scope, observations, limitations and next action; do not claim unsupported test results.

## Workshop 6 — Explain your ethical hacking portfolio in interviews (45 minutes)
**Language:** authorised, simulated, ethical, scope, outcome, learned.
**Simulation:** Answer a job-interview question about responsible conduct and accurate reporting.
**Artifact:** 90-second interview answer, distinguishing training labs from client engagements.

## Checklist
- [ ] Every demonstration is limited to fictitious or expressly authorised material.
- [ ] Every significant claim is supported by named evidence or labelled uncertain.
- [ ] Permission and reporting channels are identified before any technical activity.
- [ ] Communication is understandable to both an engineer and a manager.
- [ ] Training videos teach **English communication**, not offensive security methods.
