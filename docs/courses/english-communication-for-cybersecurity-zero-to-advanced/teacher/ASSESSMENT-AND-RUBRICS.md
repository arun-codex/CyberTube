# Assessments and Rubrics — English Communication for Cybersecurity

## What is assessed
Assess *English communication in a cybersecurity work context*: intelligible speech, comprehension, accurate paraphrasing, clear writing, factual calibration, audience sensitivity and unambiguous action requests. Do **not** assess hacking skill in this language course.

## Four-level analytic rubric (0–4 points in each of five criteria)
| Criterion | 0 — Missing/unusable | 1 — Emerging | 2 — Functional | 3 — Strong | 4 — Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning and language clarity | Unintelligible | Frequent misunderstanding | Mostly understandable | Clear minor issues | Concise and consistently clear |
| Factual fidelity | Makes up evidence | Several fact errors | Core facts usually accurate | Distinguishes facts and uncertainty | Fully traceable and calibrated |
| Audience and tone | Inappropriate | Often mismatched | Generally respectful | Appropriately adapted | Precisely adapted and empathetic |
| Structure and focus | No coherent order | Hard to follow | Basic coherent order | Logical and signposted | Highly usable at first reading/listening |
| Action and next step | No purpose/next step | Vague request | Some actionable detail | Clear action and owner | Clear, timed, verified handoff |

Maximum 20 points. For each task, record raw score out of 20; optional reporting percentage = score/20×100, rounded to nearest whole percentage point. **No official CEFR qualification or job-ready certification is conferred.**

### Completion policy proposal (owner approval required)
- Mark each module as *practiced* after the learner attempts all four lessons and submits an artifact.
- Allow resubmissions and open navigation; avoid quiz-based course unlocking.
- A suggested portfolio milestone is 14/20 or higher on two different attempts, **not a mandatory pass rule** until the course owner approves it.
- Apply the same rubric to the first baseline and final capstone for progress evidence.
- Maintain separate formative feedback and eventual administrative completion criteria.

## Baseline assessment
1. 60-second introduction.
2. Four-fact summary of a fictional spoken report.
3. 90-word incident status update.
Collect samples before instruction; do not overinterpret limited evidence.

## Milestone assessments
- **M05:** Introduce self and ask 5 relevant questions.
- **M10:** Explain 10 security words in clear English.
- **M15:** Produce an evidence-based case note and email.
- **M22:** Present a phishing or vulnerability case with proper fact/uncertainty framing.
- **M27:** Chair a meeting and deliver a structured 5-minute presentation.
- **M30:** Simulated end-to-end communication capstone.

## Capstone package
- Written intake and evidence table — 150–200 words.
- Escalation email — 120–180 words.
- Recorded analyst handover — 2 minutes (or individually approved accessible equivalent).
- Nontechnical stakeholder update — 90–120 words plus optional spoken version.
- Interview/portfolio story — 90 seconds.

## Validation / AI boundary
- Learners may use AI to receive corrections during practice, with a short disclosure of what feedback they used.
- Final graded spoken tasks should evidence the learner’s own speaking; written tasks should contain a before/after revision history where feasible.
- Educators must not rely on AI-writing detectors as sole evidence of authorship.
- No real identities, credentials or company incident data; use only synthetic examples.

## Calibration
Have two independent reviewers score one sample from low, middle and high performance; compare each criterion and write down disagreements. Adjust ambiguous anchors before publishing. Offer equivalent routes for people unable to submit audio for accessibility reasons.
