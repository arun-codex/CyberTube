# Module 23 — Reading and Summarising Threat Intelligence

> **Status:** Original draft for review; not a formally approved Agentic Course Redesign release. YouTube videos are linked, not copied or rehosted.

**Stage:** Advanced Team Communication  
**English band:** B2 (target approximation, not a certified CEFR outcome)  
**Delivery:** Video-first, 4 lessons × 35–50 minutes + 45–60 minutes of independent practice  
**Outcome:** Convert a lengthy source into an accurate, short analyst summary.

## Why a SOC analyst needs this
Summarise a publicly available threat briefing to a fictional defender; distinguish the source’s claim from your inference. This is a **communication exercise**, not a live security investigation.

## Practice language — say it, record it, improve it
- “According to the source, the campaign targets…”
- “We have not verified this activity in our environment.”
- “Based on the information currently available, I cannot confirm that conclusion.”
- “Could you confirm the next action and the person responsible?”

## Applied role-play and sample response
**Situation:** Summarise a publicly available threat briefing to a fictional defender; distinguish the source’s claim from your inference.
**Person A:** SOC analyst who must explain only the evidence available.
**Person B:** teammate, help-desk colleague, interviewer or manager who can ask for clarification.
1. Person A gives a 40–60 second explanation including who/what/when and the next action.
2. Person B asks “How do you know?” and “What is still uncertain?”
3. Person A distinguishes observation from interpretation and restates the requested action.
4. Swap roles and record one improvement.

**Example of appropriate wording (original):**
> “According to the source, the campaign targets…” “We have not verified this activity in our environment.” We will verify remaining details before drawing a conclusion.

## Additional scenario card (original)

**Title:** Threat article  
**Given evidence:** A public report describes malicious emails in an unrelated organisation.  
**Your communication assignment:** Give an attributed five-sentence summary; explain local relevance is unverified.  
**Do not invent:** anything outside the stated evidence.  
**Check:** No unattributed or copied assertions.

**Practice variation:** Give the same information twice: once in simple English for a help-desk colleague, then once in a more structured form for the SOC team. Compare clarity, facts and next actions.

## Assignment and observable completion evidence
**Submit:** Five-sentence intelligence summary with source link and caveat.

**Success emphasis:** Attribution; faithful meaning; concise synthesis. Also check that dates, counts, names and evidence are correct.
- Video watched and at least three useful English expressions noted.
- Four lesson practice records completed (audio or accessible alternative).
- Final deliverable includes known facts, unknowns, next action and intended audience where relevant.
- Provide two revisions after receiving feedback or comparing against the checklist.

## Self-assessment (0–4 for each criterion; 16 possible points)
| Criterion | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| English clarity | Unintelligible/absent | Mostly understandable; repair needed | Clear and concise for the audience |
| Factual accuracy | Fabricates or confuses facts | Most facts correct; omissions | Facts, uncertainties and evidence separated |
| Professional delivery | No audience/task awareness | Some tone/structure issues | Audience-appropriate and respectful |
| Actionability | No purpose/next step | Partly specified | Clear request/owner/next step |

**Progress rule:** This is formative evidence, not a quiz-unlock gate. Rewatch and resubmit as needed; no single score blocks course navigation.

## Safety, accessibility, and AI-use
- Use only synthetic hostnames, accounts and reserved test addresses such as `192.0.2.10` (documentation range). Never include real customer logs, credentials or private incidents.
- Respect different English accents: measure comprehensibility, not accent imitation. Offer captions, playback-speed options, transcripts when available, typed alternatives and untimed repeat attempts.
- AI may suggest language corrections during practice, but the final spoken demonstration should be the learner’s own recording or a separately documented accessibility-supported alternative. No unverified AI-generated security facts.
