# 4. Free learning resources: courses, videos, playlists and documents

The following resources form the course library. Start with the core resources; use the specialist resources only when the syllabus reaches them.

## A. Core structured courses

### 1. Pixar in a Box — The Art of Storytelling

- **Provider:** Khan Academy with Pixar.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Best for:** The shared foundations of storytelling.
- **What it covers:** Story ideas and personal perspective; character; wants and needs; obstacles; stakes; character arcs; story structure; story spine; theme; acts; visual language; film grammar; storyboarding; pitching; feedback and editing.
- **How to use it:** Complete the six-part storytelling lesson across Weeks 1–8. You do not have to finish every optional activity in one sitting; keep your own examples and story exercises.
- **Main course:** https://www.khanacademy.org/partner-content/pixar/storytelling
- **Alternate course path:** https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/storytelling
- **Educator's guide and course overview:** https://www.khanacademy.org/start/educator-guide
- **Time note:** Khan Academy's educator guide estimates about 12 working hours for the six-part story lesson in its entirety.

### 2. OpenLearn — Start Writing Fiction

- **Provider:** The Open University.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Best for:** Beginner fiction writing, character, setting and genre.
- **What it covers:** Characterisation, setting, how character and place interact, and the effect of genre.
- **How to use it:** Use during Weeks 5–8 alongside your own character and setting exercises.
- **Course:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction
- **Course content / introduction:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=1622&intro=1&section=0
- **Note:** The course is listed as a free course. A free account can be used for progress tracking and a Statement of Participation.

### 3. OpenLearn — Start Writing Fiction: Characters and Stories

- **Provider:** The Open University.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Best for:** Developing characters, keeping a writer's notebook, turning events into plot and editing.
- **What it covers:** Character creation, idea development, research, journalling, plot and reflection on writing.
- **How to use it:** Select relevant sections during Weeks 5–8. It is a companion, not a replacement for the weekly practical assignments.
- **Course:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/start-writing-fiction-characters-and-stories

### 4. Acumen Academy — Storytelling for Change

- **Provider:** Acumen Academy.
- **Cost:** Listed as free; on-demand; six-week course.
- **Best for:** Personal storytelling, leadership presence, expressing a story, presentations and feedback.
- **What it includes:** 15 videos, six reading guides and five workshop guides.
- **How to use it:** Work through selected sections during Weeks 13–16. You can continue the course beyond this masterclass if you want to complete every guide.
- **Course and enrolment:** https://acumenacademy.org/course/storytelling-change
- **Topics listed in its syllabus:** Introduction to storytelling; story examples; life-map exercise; leadership presence; identifying your story; story content and form; expressiveness; vocal range; presentation planning and feedback.

### 5. OpenLearn — Talk the Talk

- **Provider:** The Open University.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Best for:** Presentations, speeches, persuasive oral communication and delivery.
- **What it covers:** Analysing examples, connecting ideas through language, creating and practising a presentation.
- **How to use it:** Use the relevant lessons during Weeks 14 and 16.
- **Course:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/talk-the-talk

### 6. OpenLearn — Creative Writing and Critical Reading

- **Provider:** The Open University.
- **Cost:** Free.
- **Best for:** Advanced analysis and revision.
- **What it covers:** Critical reading, learning from other writers and examples from fiction, creative nonfiction, poetry and scriptwriting.
- **How to use it:** Use during Weeks 19–20 as an advanced extension. It is not the first course to start with.
- **Course:** https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing-and-critical-reading

### TED-Ed Public Speaking 101: recommended lesson order

Use the [full collection](https://ed.ted.com/ted_ed_collections/public-speaking-101). It contains 11 lessons. Prioritise these topics as you reach Weeks 13–16:

1. How to uncover your best ideas.
2. What happens when you share an idea?
3. 4 ways to tell a great story.
4. How to explain something complicated.
5. 6 ways to be more persuasive.
6. How to present your ideas effectively.
7. How to effectively use visuals in your presentation.

The collection may display additional lessons or reorder them. Follow the topic names rather than relying on a fixed position in the playlist.

## C. Additional specialist channels and playlists

Use these after you understand the basics. Do not binge-watch them instead of completing assignments.

- **Lessons from the Screenplay:** https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCErSSa3CaP_GJxmFpdjG9Jw  
  Film-script analysis: examine why a scene, character decision or structure works.
- **StudioBinder:** https://www.youtube.com/@StudioBinder  
  Film grammar, shots, screenwriting and visual storytelling. The three-act video linked above is a good starting point.
- **Brandon Sanderson's official writing advice page:** https://www.brandonsanderson.com/pages/writing-advice  
  Links to university lecture playlists and writing resources. His lectures focus strongly on science fiction and fantasy, so treat them as a specialist fiction resource rather than a complete all-round storytelling course.
- **Brandon Sanderson's 2014 BYU writing lectures:** https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLRO9W1Nmh6clZP-IAhMeMpMru7vJaW7KJ  
- **Brandon Sanderson's 2016 BYU writing lectures:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?list=PLH3mK1NZn9QqOSj3ObrP3xL8tEJQ12-vL&v=N4ZDBOc2tX8  
- **The Moth:** https://www.themoth.org/  
  A major source of real-life spoken storytelling examples.

## D. Hindi-language reinforcement searches

The main structured course material above is mostly in English. These links open YouTube searches, not endorsements of a particular teacher or fixed course. Preview the instructor, check the quality of explanations and compare advice with the core curriculum.

- [Hindi: story writing, kahani writing and story structure](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=kahani+likhna+story+writing+in+hindi+story+structure)
- [Hindi: character creation and plot development](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=character+creation+plot+development+story+writing+hindi)
- [Hindi: storytelling and kahani sunane ki technique](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=kahani+sunane+ki+technique+storytelling+hindi)
- [Hindi: public speaking, voice modulation and narration](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=public+speaking+voice+modulation+storytelling+hindi)
- [Hindi: screenplay and visual storytelling](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=screenplay+writing+visual+storytelling+hindi)

**Quality filter for Hindi videos:** Prefer lessons that explain a concept, demonstrate it with an example and give you an exercise. Avoid videos that only promise “secret formulas”, “viral storytelling hacks” or guaranteed audience retention.

## E. Free tools for practising

- **Google Docs:** https://docs.google.com/ — drafts, outlines, feedback and version history.
- **Google Drive:** https://drive.google.com/ — store scripts, recordings and storyboards.
- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/ — watch reference stories; upload practice videos privately or unlisted if you need to review them.
- **Audacity:** https://www.audacityteam.org/ — free audio recording and editing for spoken-story practice.
- **Canva storyboard templates:** https://www.canva.com/storyboards/templates/ — optional storyboard layouts. Free and paid design elements may be mixed; do not purchase anything for this course.
- **Obsidian:** https://obsidian.md/ — optional local notes and a searchable story idea bank; free for personal use.

You do not need all these tools. A notebook, phone voice recorder and one document folder are enough.
